# Candidates — Warning Label for Humans: One Line, Big Laugh

Judge brief (in order): (1) a single-line safety warning label for every human, funny enough to laugh at; (2) the laugh lands in one read, turn in the last few words; (3) recognition — true about people in general, not random absurdity; (4) sounds like a real printed label in wording and rhythm; (5) inside 160 characters, no filler, no second idea, no repeated joke.

## 1 — 130 chars
> WARNING: This human contains no user manual, no warranty, and no off switch. Operates entirely on caffeine, confidence, and vibes.

Rationale: Perfect label form (WARNING + contents + operating note) and a very funny, very true closing triplet. Weakness: two triplets means the laugh is slightly smeared across the line rather than one clean turn; "vibes" as the final word is the punch but the line carries two joke beats.

## 2 — 101 chars
> WARNING: This human runs on 8% knowledge and 92% confidence. Do not shake. Do not ask for directions.

Rationale: Razor-sharp recognition (Dunning–Kruger, everyone knows this person) and textbook label rhythm. Weakness: the core joke lands mid-line ("92% confidence"); the closing "Do not ask for directions" is a second, smaller joke, so the turn is not cleanly in the last few words.

## 3 — 104 chars ⭐ WINNER
> WARNING: This human will only read the instructions after breaking the thing. Warranty void since birth.

Rationale: Single idea, one clean turn. The punch — "Warranty void since birth" — lands in the last three words, exactly as criterion 2 asks. Recognition is universal (everyone has skipped the manual and broken something first). "Warranty void since birth" is pure printed-label language (criterion 4), and the whole line is label, no filler, no second joke (criterion 5).

## 4 — 107 chars
> CAUTION: Contents may suddenly believe they are right. Keep away from facts, mirrors, and comment sections.

Rationale: Good label voice and a nice late turn on "comment sections". Weakness: "believe they are right" is a slightly weaker, more abstract laugh than the concrete truths in 1–3; "mirrors" is a second small joke in the middle.

## 5 — 93 chars
> WARNING: This human is 70% water and 30% unfinished business. Handle with sarcasm and snacks.

Rationale: Cute, label-like, fun closer. Weakness: "unfinished business" is vague and less instantly recognizable than the others; the laugh is mild rather than a real turn.

## 6 — 103 chars
> WARNING: Do not operate while sleep-deprived, emotionally available, or within 50 feet of a group chat.

Rationale: Strong label grammar and a good final turn ("group chat"). Weakness: "emotionally available" reads oddly in a warning label and muddies the recognition; the middle of the list does little work.

## 7 — 112 chars
> CAUTION: This human may spontaneously start a podcast. Do not feed after midnight. Do not ask about the startup.

Rationale: "Do not feed after midnight" is a great label callback. Weakness: the podcast joke is narrow — not true of people in general (criterion 3) — and the line ends on a second closing beat rather than one turn.

## 8 — 126 chars
> WARNING: Contents under pressure may produce opinions, tears, or both at once. This is normal. This is fine. This is not fine.

Rationale: The triple beat is funny on first read. Weakness: it leans on the "this is fine" meme and repeats the joke three times, violating criterion 5 (no repeated joke, no second idea); the turn is smeared, not landed.

## Winner
Candidate 3 — the strongest on every criterion in order: unmistakably a warning label for humans, one read, punch in the last three words, universally true, real label wording, 104 characters with nothing wasted.
